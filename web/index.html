Three kinds of style, one dial

Color grades

A reference photo mixes the scene with the grade. Copying its colors directly copies the scene too — that is why a beach reference turns your forest yellow. lookcam estimates the grade alone and stores it as a 3D lookup table, which also opens in Lightroom or Resolve.

under 1 second · no GPU

Camera character

What makes a 2006 compact look like one is not its colors: it is chroma noise, smeared detail sharpened into halos, purple fringing, JPEG blocks and an orange date stamp. Those are modeled directly, so the artifacts are real rather than painted on.

0.05–1 second · no GPU

Reimagine

For an anime painting or a toy diorama, colors are not enough — the scene has to be repainted. FLUX.2 klein does that on the GPU box at 1 MP, then a 4× upscaler brings it back to sensor resolution.

15–25 seconds · GPU box

Faces stay recognizable

Diffusion redraws a face into a person, not the person. Identity lives in fine structure — the exact shape and spacing of eyes, nose and mouth — while style lives in color and broad tone. So faces are detected and given back the photo's own structure under a soft mask, keeping the style everywhere else.

Original · anime · face restoration at 0.5, 0.7 and 1.0

Live preview, two layers

Live diffusion tops out near 4 frames per second on an 8 GB card, so the viewfinder does not wait for it. Every frame gets a local stand-in; real diffusion frames arrive a few times a second and re-teach the stand-in its colors. Hold the camera still and the real frame fades in.
